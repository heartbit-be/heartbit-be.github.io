The primary audience is recruiters and professional acquaintances checking Laurens's capabilities and suitability for an engagement. A secondary purpose is a professional homepage to share on business cards.

Laurens describes his capabilities as full-stack software engineering, development leadership, development coaching, technical architecture, cloud architecture, DevOps, AI engineering, and AI coaching. Today his role is senior engineer/development lead with architecture responsibilities; he wants greater architecture responsibility in future engagements.

The desired work combines cloud architecture, coaching a development team, and hands-on technical delivery. AI engineer is also an explicit desired match. A narrowly constrained role, such as DevOps implementation alone, is undesirable. Laurens recognises himself in an introduction as a freelance senior software engineer and technical lead who turns complex requirements into working software, shapes cloud architecture, and helps teams improve delivery, but finds that wording stiff. Final public wording remains open.

Experience includes Azure, .NET, Angular, Azure DevOps, and PostgreSQL. Desired growth areas include architecture, developer coaching, and AI coaching. Engagements must be at least partially remote or very close to home. The public location is Tienen, Belgium; communicate a preference for remote work without publishing a detailed onsite policy.

The simpler, first-person introduction is preferred, with “freelance” and location removed from the introduction. This is a copy preference, not a change to employment status or the previously supplied location. Working draft: “I'm Laurens, a software engineer and technical lead. I like figuring out how a system should fit together, building it, and helping the team along the way. My work spans full-stack development, cloud architecture, and putting AI to use in software delivery.”

Current professional AI experience is using coding agents to deliver software. Building AI-powered applications is planned, not established experience. Laurens wants enquiries both for helping developers adopt coding agents and for designing and delivering AI-powered applications, with more real-world experience in the former. Laurens has also helped friends and family use AI and follows its possibilities in broader business contexts. Final public wording remains open.

Developer coaching is part of how Laurens works in a team. He follows up with developers, listens to problems, and helps resolve them through pair programming, useful information, and guidance. Do not present this as a separate coaching package without a new decision.

Heartbit is the company umbrella. Laurens is also starting a business selling physical products made with 3D printing, sometimes combined with other materials, under a completely separate identity. The site could link to webshops and Etsy stores. He hopes this business can also demonstrate entrepreneurial capability to professional visitors. Do not claim commercial success.

Heartbit should also accept custom 3D-printing enquiries: printing existing models, designing from someone's idea, prototypes, and replacement parts. The separate product identity may accept custom work as well; Heartbit can be an additional contact point or relay. These offers can coexist.

Do not show the separate shop until it is live. Keep its future place in the content plan.

Leave the blog out of the first version. Laurens does not particularly enjoy blogging; future work-related writing remains optional.

## Content structure and contact

Three main pages are agreed:

- Home: introduction, capabilities, working with teams, and contact.
- Experience: selected past roles and contributions, view/download CV, optional application renewal case study. The case study should not be prominent on the homepage.
- 3D printing: service scope, a real example, and an enquiry link. Add links to the separate shops here once live.

Email and LinkedIn should be easy to find throughout. Do not stretch the content across separate About, Services, Work, Shops, and Contact pages.

Laurens answered yes to email as the main contact action with LinkedIn as a secondary route, and explicitly wants CV viewing/downloading. Confirm actual CV content and the LinkedIn destination before using them.

Laurens will supply his CV later and has authorised dummy content for now. Use clearly marked illustrative entries and CV placeholders, not invented employers, dates, or qualifications presented as real history. The repository currently contains neither a CV nor a LinkedIn destination.

## Experience example: application renewal

A deadline-driven proof of concept was put into production under management pressure without time to mature the solution. Users subsequently encountered issues, and its technical choices prevented some desired features.

Laurens gathered user problems and feature requests, produced a functional analysis and technical analysis covering cloud architecture, domain model, and API surface, persuaded leadership to invest in renewal, and owned and implemented end-to-end delivery of the replacement application.

Laurens confirms he was the sole implementer. He is open to an anonymous case study but does not want it front and center on the homepage.

Owner-reported outcomes: improved security and user experience; previously blocked, highly requested features became possible; the replacement was built for extension and scale and opened to external clients instead of being internal-only. No numerical impact or measured scaling results have been supplied. Do not turn the intended resolution of all issues into a claim that all issues were demonstrably resolved.

## Printing example: feeding-pump holder

Parents of a young child using a feeding pump wanted an easy-to-use holder for both the pump and its bottle or container. They needed it to fit in a small backpack for feeding on the go and attach easily to a crib or bed for nighttime feeds.

Laurens designed, prototyped, and delivered a multi-part 3D-printed assembly. Photos have not been supplied. Proposed copy should describe the design and delivery without inventing validation, measured outcomes, or identifying details.
